Correct AI Usage

My Original Paragraph

I think AI can be used correctly in college by generating practice quizzes, explaining lessons in simpler terms, or using programs like Claude or ChatGPT to brainstorm AI essay structures and receive feedback on your writing. In the workplace, AI can draft templates for professional emails, summarize meeting transcripts, sort through huge data sets for trends, and much more. AI helps streamline administrative tasks and analyzes patterns, allowing workers to focus more on higher-level strategy. In fields such as healthcare, law, & finance, AI must only be used for back-end efficiency. AI assists doctors by flagging potential abnormalities in medical imaging for review, or by compiling case-law summaries for legal researchers to verify manually.

AI Evaluation and Critique

I agree with your explanation of how AI can be used responsibly in college and the workplace. You make a strong point that AI should support learning by helping students understand difficult concepts, practice material, and improve their writing rather than completing assignments for them. Your workplace examples also demonstrate how AI can save time and allow employees to focus on more important responsibilities. However, I would suggest clarifying that AI is not necessarily limited to back-end tasks in industries such as healthcare, law, and finance. AI can also support professional decision-making, provided that qualified individuals review its outputs and remain responsible for final decisions. Additionally, protecting confidential information and verifying AI-generated content are essential parts of using these tools ethically.

Benefits of AI

  1. Improves efficiency by automating repetitive tasks.
  2. Helps students understand difficult concepts.

Who Benefits?

  1. Students and educators
  2. Businesses and employees

How AI Tools Used

Incorrect AI Usage

Examples of Incorrect AI Usage

1. Academic Dishonesty
Using AI to complete entire assignments or exams and submitting the work as your own without permission or acknowledgment.

2. Spreading Misinformation
Sharing AI-generated information without checking whether it is accurate or comes from reliable sources.

3. Violating Privacy
Entering confidential student records, personal information, or private documents into AI tools without authorization.

My Opinion on Incorrect AI Usage

I believe AI can be a helpful tool, but it becomes harmful when people use it dishonestly. Students should use AI to help with their learning rather than use it to complete their work. It is important to check AI-generated information because it is not always accurate. Using AI responsibly helps maintain academic integrity and protects personal information.
